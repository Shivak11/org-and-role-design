Three AI skills for designing organisations and roles when people and AI share the work

You install the plugin in Claude Code or Codex, or copy a skill folder into another assistant, and then ask the assistant to run a skill with you. Each skill works through a conversation about your own work and keeps a record of your choices.

What do you want to do?

Redesign a team's work for people and AI agents

You choose one workflow your team runs often. The assistant draws the current hierarchy, then goes through the workflow step by step and asks whether AI could do each step and, if not, why. It shows each position's work split into the part AI or software would do and the part a person still does. With that split in front of you, you decide who takes the remaining judgements and how many posts the work needs. You make every choice; the assistant records it.

You bring
Your role, one recurring workflow and the people who do it, by role title. No employee records are needed.
It takes
About 30 to 45 minutes.
You get
Current and proposed views of the hierarchy, the work split, reporting lines and decision authority, with each agent shown next to the person who supervises it, a workbook of every choice, and a limited trial with an owner, a measure and a stopping rule.
Where it stops
It ends with a proposal. It does not connect to company systems or make any personnel decision.

Learn organisation structure from a workflow you redesign

The learner picks a refund, a purchase request, a new employee joining or a workflow of their own, and writes how it runs today. They then propose one change. The assistant keeps the before and after sequences visible and asks short questions about who decides, what information they use and who reports to whom. Only after the learner has made their choices does the assistant name the concepts those choices show.

The learner brings
A workflow they can describe, real or imagined.
It takes
About 20 minutes to develop the workflow and 5 to 10 minutes to discuss it in class.
The learner gets
Their before and after workflow, an explanation of the structure concepts it demonstrates, one risk with a way to check it, and a short record they can take into design-human-ai-org.
Where it stops
It ends with the redesigned structure and does not assign any work to AI.

What happened before

  1. The customer gives the receipt to an associate.
  2. The associate checks the purchase record and messages the manager.
  3. The manager decides every refund.
  4. Accounts pays.

Your completed redesign

  1. The customer gives the receipt to an associate.
  2. The associate checks the receipt and purchase record.
  3. Below Rs 2,000 the associate decides; above it the manager decides.
  4. Accounts pays.

The decision on small refunds has moved to the associate, which decentralises it. The written limit standardises and formalises the decision. The manager still has six direct reports, so the span of control is unchanged.

A shortened version of the worked example included with the skill.

Turn a job description into a skill map and a course

You paste or upload a job description. The assistant identifies the role, level and discipline, keeps only the skills the employer would reject a candidate for lacking, and sorts them into four pillars. If you ask for a course, it builds modules of eleven screens each, moving from concepts to scenarios, people problems and a written case, and ends with a mock interview script.

You bring
One job description.
You get
A skill map in JSON with 8 to 12 skills, each with a difficulty, a weight, a description of how it shows up in this job and a sample interview question, plus two glossaries of terms. On request, a full course in JSON.
Where it stops
It looks at one role at a time and does not look at how roles fit together in a team.

Using two skills in sequence

  1. A class runs run-org-structure-exercise on a workflow and learns the structure concepts from its own redesign.
  2. Each group copies the closing record into design-human-ai-org, which starts from that workflow and asks which steps AI could take on.
