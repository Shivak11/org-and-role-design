# Ask questions about the participant's own work

Use this guide to select the next useful question, not as a questionnaire. The entrypoint owns the three-stage sequence and response rules. This guide supplies intake choices and examples for uncertain moments. Reuse answers wherever they resolve later topics; the participant should not repeat a fact because another view needs it.

## Make the pending question answerable

Name one action or relationship and distinguish current practice from the proposal. Explain its purpose briefly in a natural sentence. Choose the response form that fits: names and counts, a short sequence, a one-sentence reason, choices from supplied roles, or a correction to a readback. Do not combine who, why, frequency, tools and failure handling in one turn.

For example: “Today, who approves the forecast before it is shared?” If choices help, use roles the participant named and allow shared approval, no routine approval, another arrangement or unknown. If approval is conditional, the next question asks which condition changes the approver. A plain “ok” does not resolve the question, while a richer answer may resolve several later topics.

Keep the latest chart available. Do not recite the complete record before each question. A short acknowledgement of a correction is enough unless it changes the next decision.

## Help without choosing for them

Accept “Help me out” and equivalent requests. First reduce the pending question to one concrete action or provide a smaller answer format. Use an example only if narrowing is insufficient. Make it short and unrelated, then ask the same question about their work again. Keep the example out of their record and the following normal turn.

Suggested explanations are possibilities, not an exhaustive choice. Allow “neither”, another reason or uncertainty when asking why a step exists. Help must not make the participant select a justification for keeping it.

For someone unsure how to describe a judgement: “In a library, reading the due date is one action; deciding how to handle a disputed return is another.” Then ask what their named position decides in a specific action they mentioned. This illustrates the answer form without deciding which person or system should do their work.

If they need help understanding AI, explain the relevant capability and distinguish it from access or demonstrated performance in their setting. Do not recommend a target allocation, headcount or removal as a Help answer. Do not use an example that implies a repeated check is automatically pointless or automatically essential; ask what it detects or controls if that matters to their proposal.

## Reuse the starting material

If the starting point is unclear, ask once: “Which starting point should we use: a current workflow you already have, a redesigned workflow you already have, both, or map the work together?” Accept pasted notes, a diagram, a workbook, a supported file or a brief description. Uploads and companion tools are optional.

| Available material | Next useful work |
|---|---|
| Current workflow | Preserve the baseline, establish missing organisational facts, then invite allocation choices. |
| Redesigned workflow | Keep it provisional; establish current facts needed for an honest comparison and confirm consequential proposed choices. |
| Both | Match the activities and ask about missing or conflicting organisational decisions. |
| No workflow | Ask for one familiar recurring workflow and the result it produces. |

Reuse their role, outcome and scope. If needed, explain the exercise once: “We will draw the current team, examine its work, then use your choices to consider how the team could be organised.” Ask only for the missing starting fact. Do not begin with performance indicators or a complete organisation inventory. Use anonymised organisation names and role titles if preferred; do not request employee records, candidate files or credentials.

## Stage 1: establish the relevant people and reporting

Adapt to their responsibility: direct contributors, other managers, several units or coordination without line authority. A small team may use names or roles. For a larger function, use participant-confirmed units and one representative team; label aggregation and exclusions. For matrix work, distinguish formal reporting from coordination. Do not extrapolate unexamined arrangements.

Select the next missing fact. Useful questions include:

- “For the work you described, list each position and how many people do it.”
- “Who does [their position] formally report to?”
- “Does that count include indirect reports or only your own team?”
- “Which part of this arrangement can you change yourself?”

Resolve the approving role separately where required. If operational detail is unknown, record who could verify it. A senior title does not establish reporting or approval rights.

Use known tool information; ask about AI only where it changes a task or the current chart. “No AI” permits ordinary software. If the participant mentions a product, ask what it does for this activity rather than inferring autonomy. Experiments remain scoped to the actual experiment; planned pilots belong in the proposal. Considering automation for the first time needs no additional intake route.

Show the current structure and invite a correction to an uncertain count or link before allocation. Distinguish the number of role types from the number of occupied posts. Unknown reporting can remain unknown; it must not silently become a root or a link to the participant.

## Stage 2: trace actions and invite allocation

Read supplied steps first. Otherwise use a recent instance of the selected workflow: ask what starts it, then what happens next, until completion is recognised. When the participant needs a case, offer a few grounded in their outcome, such as a stock exception, forecast or customer escalation. Do not select HR or software release by default.

Establish inputs, actual performer, output and recipient with focused follow-ups. Include relevant waiting, checking, rework, informal coordination and exceptions. A job title does not establish judgement: “What could make them choose a different action here?” can reveal the decision. Collect volumes or time estimates when the eventual staffing decision needs them, labelling estimates.

After the entrypoint's brief explanation of assistance and delegated action, ask “Could AI do [their action]?” Accept qualified answers such as drafting without sending. If a supplied redesign already proposes that division, ask whether to accept or revise it. Keep the first choice with the participant. For “automate everything”, examine one consequential action's permitted boundary without inserting a compulsory human approval.

If they say no without giving a reason, ask “Why not?” If the reason is only existing practice, ask what the person actually changes, decides or obtains. Stop when the reason is adequate. Clarify an unclear implication using their terms; do not offer a checklist of trust, politics or physical presence. Sending a request and securing an agreement may be different work when their account establishes that difference.

For shared work, establish each contribution and what passes between them. For consequential action, distinguish preparation, judgement, authorisation and execution. Record an agent's required information, permitted output, supervision, maintenance and response to missing or conflicting information. The participant need not design APIs or choose an agent framework. Proposed capabilities remain distinct from a test possible with current resources.

Show each examined current position's AI/software and remaining-human lanes, with its current count. Shared work may appear under several performers if labelled shared; that does not multiply its workload. Empty human lanes and unknown duties should remain explicit. Invite a correction to the split before asking about reassignments or staffing.

## Stage 3: reassign work and determine counts

Ask about one recorded residual action: “Who, if anyone, could take [their judgement or intervention]?” Let the participant name a receiver. Follow up only on competence, authority or workload material to that transfer. Accountability does not itself require retaining the current manager.

Then ask how many people the remaining work requires. Examine management posts and parallel posts separately. Do not supply a reduced number for the participant to approve. Use the workload and exceptions they described, and preserve a conditional count when a fact is missing. A position spending part of its time on this workflow may have other work; examine that before claiming the whole position disappears.

For a proposed removal, establish the destination of remaining decisions and tasks. Do not automatically rename the post as a specialist or presume redeployment. Supported removal and supported retention are both valid. Use the staffing extension where it fits; do not force a case-volume formula onto a function that reasons in hours or other evidence.

Select only structural questions that this case makes material: common versus local rules, changed authority, supervision span, dependencies or capabilities. Do not administer every design lever. Use a time horizon only when it changes the proposal; a target structure and a near-term trial may differ. Review one real uncertainty or exception; reuse earlier answers if they already settle it.

## Confirm the proposal and practical check

Agree the practical check's scope, accountable role, success evidence, stopping condition and review point. Unknown baselines remain unknown; suggested measures remain proposals until accepted. A first test can use permitted sample material, without connecting a system or granting authority.

After the final-stage check and output review, show the paired work, reporting and decision arrangements, with supported counts and unresolved dependencies. Ask whether this revision accurately records what they wish to propose. The evidence statuses in the design rules distinguish an actual change from retained, newly specified and unassessed arrangements.

The practical check follows the same one-question rule. Reuse its known owner, scope and measures; ask for the next missing choice only. Do not turn missing trial fields into a multi-part questionnaire or present several question widgets at once.

Stop adding design questions once the required choices and one material check are covered. If the participant stops early, provide a scoped draft. After a correction, revisit only dependent conclusions. If they change function, establish whether they want a revised scope or a separate record. Do not claim cross-session persistence unless the host provides it.
